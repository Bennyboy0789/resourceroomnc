Our Individualized SAT Prep Process
Every student starts in a different place, so every plan we build is different.
We begin with a comprehensive evaluation and a diagnostic approach. Full-length proctored diagnostics let us establish a student’s current baseline, identify strengths and weaknesses section by section, and make informed decisions about exactly how tutoring time should be spent. From there we build a plan around the actual student in front of us instead of forcing that student into a rigid, one-size-fits-all program.
Some students need remediation in a specific content area, whether that is algebra, grammar, or reading comprehension. Others are already strong academically but need help with timing, focus, strategy, or stamina. Many need a mix of both, plus support with the confidence and pacing that make such a difference on test day. Our one-to-one instruction is built to respond to all of these students and everything in between, and diagnostic check-ins continue throughout the program so we can adjust as the student grows.
This individualized model is especially valuable for students who are below grade level, students who struggle with math or with reading and writing, students who deal with test anxiety, and students who benefit from more direct support with focus, organization, and endurance. Throughout the program a student works with the same consistent tutor, has access to online lessons, homework, quizzes, and review materials, and follows a plan paced to their priorities and goals.
1. Diagnose
Initial full-length diagnostics establish a clear score baseline and reveal section-by-section strengths and weaknesses.
2. Plan
We build a targeted plan around the student’s profile, whether they need content remediation, strategy, pacing, or confidence.
3. Teach
One consistent tutor delivers customized instruction matched to the student’s pace, learning style, and target score.
4. Monitor
Ongoing practice and periodic proctored assessments track progress and keep prep on course through test day.
What the SAT Covers
The current SAT is a digital exam with two sections, Reading & Writing and Math, and runs about 2 hours and 14 minutes plus breaks.
Reading & Writing
Comprehension, vocabulary in context, grammar, revision, and editing skills.
Math
Algebra, advanced math, problem-solving and data analysis, and geometry and trigonometry. A calculator is allowed on the entire Math section, and Bluebook includes a built-in one.
Because the digital SAT is shorter, adaptive, and strategically paced, students benefit from prep that teaches both the content and the mechanics of the exam. Before test day, students should download Bluebook and complete exam setup on their device in advance.

SAT Prep Questions, Answered
SAT or ACT, which is better?
There is no universal answer, but most students we work with currently prefer the SAT. Many find its pacing more manageable, while the ACT’s timing is often more unforgiving. We recommend starting with a diagnostic and letting the data decide which test fits your student best.
How many hours of tutoring does a student need?
Most students do best with our 20-hour private package, which gives enough time to build on strengths, address weak areas, improve timing, and build confidence. We confirm the right amount during your free consultation after reviewing goals and diagnostics.
Do you offer in-person and virtual tutoring?
Yes. Private SAT tutoring is available both in-person at our Holly Springs center and virtually, with the same personalized instruction and quality either way.
Who is private SAT tutoring best for?
Students who want a customized plan, have uneven skills across sections, are below grade level in math or reading and writing, deal with test anxiety, need help with pacing or focus, benefit from individualized instruction, or have busy schedules that need flexibility.
When do the SAT group classes run?
Our group SAT class is offered once a year in preparation for the March SAT, traditionally one of the most popular test dates. The next group cycle begins in January 2027. If you want structured prep in a classroom setting, reach out early to reserve a spot. If you need prep on a different timeline, our private tutoring is available year-round.
How much can a student improve?
It depends on where a student starts. Most of our students gain between 200 and 300 points from their baseline through focused preparation, strategy work, and consistent practice. Students who begin with lower scores often see the largest jumps, frequently climbing toward the 300-point range, while students who start higher tend to land in the 200-point range. Your free consultation and initial diagnostic give us a realistic target for your student.
